Audio — the same transformer, on sound

Nothing in the transformer knows about words. It knows about integers and their order. A codec turns a waveform into integers fifty times a second, and then the whole stack — pretraining, the KV cache, the sampler, quantization, LoRA — works on sound without being told. Read chapter 20.

The bitrate ladder

Residual VQ quantizes what the previous codebook got wrong, so the prefix of a code is a valid code: decode fewer codebooks and stop. Bitrate becomes a dial you turn at decode time rather than a property of the checkpoint. Play these in order against the original — this is the same trade quantization makes silently in the weights, except here you can hear it.

Is the codebook actually being used?

Codebook collapse is router collapse again: a handful of entries win early, the rest never train, reconstruction plateaus — and the loss curve looks fine. This is the panel that sees it. Perplexity is the effective codebook size, so 40 out of 1,024 means 96% of the capacity was thrown away.

What a second of sound actually is

The claim of the phase, as a grid of integers. Every column is one frame; every row is one codebook. A language model over this pays one position per column, and eight heads per position.

Audio runs

Codec and audio-LM runs publish the same pid / STOP / log contract as every other trainer here, so scripts/stop.sh and the dashboard drive them unchanged. Each writes a reconstruction into samples/ as it trains — a codec's loss does not tell you whether it is intelligible, and a three-second file does.
