Audio — the same transformer, on sound

Nothing in the transformer knows about words. It knows about integers and their order. A codec turns a waveform into integers fifty times a second, and then the whole stack — pretraining, the KV cache, the sampler, quantization, LoRA — works on sound without being told. Read chapter 20.

waveform16,000/s → conv encoder320× down → residual VQ8 codebooks → 8 integers50 times a second → conv decoder320× up → waveform

The bitrate ladder

Residual VQ quantizes what the previous codebook got wrong, so the prefix of a code is a valid code: decode fewer codebooks and stop. Bitrate becomes a dial you turn at decode time rather than a property of the checkpoint. Play these in order against the original — this is the same trade quantization makes silently in the weights, except here you can hear it.

Is the codebook actually being used?

Codebook collapse is router collapse again: a handful of entries win early, the rest never train, reconstruction plateaus — and the loss curve looks fine. This is the panel that sees it. Perplexity is the effective codebook size, so 40 out of 1,024 means 96% of the capacity was thrown away.

What a second of sound actually is

The claim of the phase, as a grid of integers. Every column is one frame; every row is one codebook. A language model over this pays one position per column, and eight heads per position.

Does the codec carry other voices?

A codec trained on one reader may only know how to rebuild that reader. This compares clips of its own voice it never trained on with LibriSpeech speakers it never heard: MCD at each bitrate, and one recogniser's word error rate on the original and on the reconstruction of the same clip. Pick data/asr/test-clean in the ladder above to hear it.

Text to speech — in whose voice, and can you still understand it?

The audio language model reads a sentence and writes codec codes, which the codec turns back into sound. Each report has the recogniser transcribe the original recording, the codec's copy of it, and the TTS output of the same sentence: one judge, three rows. Voice is the speaker model's cosine between the TTS and that speaker's own recording, read against the codec's copy (the ceiling) and two real recordings of one speaker. Cloned means a few seconds of the speaker's recording were given as a prompt; embedding means only their voice fingerprint. Read chapter 21.

Compare two reports, sentence by sentence

20–40 sentences make totals noisy, so the comparison counts sentences: on how many did b come out with fewer word errors, and on how many closer to the speaker's voice. A cloned report is offered against its control (same sentences, same reference, no prompt).
