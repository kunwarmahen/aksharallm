Dictation — speech in, text out, and still right on day two

A from-scratch speech recogniser: it turns 10 ms slices of sound into a picture (log-mel), a Conformer reads that picture, and every 40 ms it guesses one letter — or blank, meaning "nothing new here". Read the chapter.

your voice16,000 samples/s → Conformera letter or blank, 25/s → beam searchword LM + your dictionary → cleanuppunctuation tagger, fillers, commands → texttyped where your cursor is

Every piece is ours. The cleanup is a tagger: it labels each word with the punctuation after it and how it is capitalised, and cannot write a word of its own — so cleanup can never put words in your mouth. The day-two checks below count that.

No recogniser yet. The smoke run needs no download and takes a few minutes: 
.venv/bin/python -m aksharallm.audio corpus --out data/audio/synth-asr --clips 400
scripts/audio.sh asr-synth
It only knows five vowel names. For one that knows English, train on LibriSpeech: 
.venv/bin/python -m aksharallm.asr fetch train-clean-100 && .venv/bin/python -m aksharallm.asr pack train-clean-100
scripts/audio.sh asr-libri100

Dictate

Press, talk, press again. What comes back is finished text: punctuated, capitalised, ums removed. Say new line, new paragraph or scratch that (which deletes the sentence you are in). If a word is wrong, fix it in the box and press Teach it — that is how it learns your names.

Try the cleanup without speaking

Type words the way the recogniser writes them — lower case, no punctuation, fillers and commands included — and see what cleanup makes of them. Your dictionary's spellings and replacements apply, as they would to speech. Same as python -m aksharallm.dictate clean "…".

What it has learned from you

One correction puts a word in your dictionary (the beam search leans towards it) and remembers your spelling of it (GitHub, not Github). The same fix twice makes a replacement — heard "sean", write "shaun" — but never between two ordinary words: "their" → "there" is a grammar fix, not a rule for every sentence.

Your voice, as a test set

Every other number on this page is audiobooks read by strangers. Read these thirty sentences once each and your voice becomes a corpus like any other — scored with the same WER, beside test-clean. One sentence per recording: press, read just the sentence shown, press again — it moves to the next by itself, and a take that runs much longer than its sentence is refused. A bad take (a cough, a door) can simply be recorded again. Recordings stay on this machine, in data/asr/my-voice/.

Keys: R start / stop a take · ← → previous / next sentence (not while typing in a box).

python -m aksharallm.asr myvoice record [--set train]   # the same, from a terminal
scripts/audio.sh asr-me                                   # adapt to your voice (Dashboard: asr-me)

How noise hurts it

The same test-clean sentences with real recorded noise mixed in (DEMAND: a kitchen, a café, a street, a car… — sounds the recogniser never heard in training), at a fixed loudness relative to the voice. 20 dB is a quiet room, 10 dB a busy café, 0 dB noise as loud as you. Other people talking is the hardest; a car's low rumble barely matters, because speech does not use those frequencies.
